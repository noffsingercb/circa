Where the history comes from, what built it, and who made it.

The history

Wikidata
The structured backbone: events, dates, coordinates and the links between them. Released under CC0, which is why a project like this can exist at all.
Wikipedia
The prose. Where a tile has a sentence explaining what happened, it generally started here. Text is available under CC BY-SA.
GeoHistory
The dataset and the engine underneath Circa. It decides how far each event reaches, how much it mattered, and which events earn a place in a timeline. Circa is a front end over it and nothing more.

The record is broad rather than complete, and thinner the further back and the further from large cities you go. It is a good starting point and a poor last word.

The build

Circa
This site and the app. A static bundle over a small read-only API — no accounts, no database of its own, nothing to sign in to.
SvelteKit
The app. Prerendered to static files, which is why it loads the way it does.

Who made it

Circa was designed and built by Ben Noffsinger, along with the GeoHistory dataset and engine behind it. There is a longer version of the story on Why this exists.

Notion AI was a working part of how it got built rather than a footnote — a collaborator on the architecture, the scoring model, the copy on these pages, and a fair amount of the code, worked through in conversation and tracked on a Notion board alongside it.

Using it elsewhere

Circa runs as a chromeless embed at /embed, sized to whatever page it is dropped into. If you want it on your own site, that is the address to point an iframe at.
